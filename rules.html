01

Confirmed framework

Authority and scope

All SATAACK 50 matches will be governed by the official playing conditions issued by the league. Competition notices and venue instructions may add operational requirements for a particular match or stage.

The latest officially published rulebook will take precedence over summaries, promotional material and earlier drafts.

02

Details to be confirmed

Player eligibility

Every player must complete the official registration and verification process, provide accurate information and participate only under their registered identity.

  • Eligibility criteria, including applicable age and residency requirements
  • Registration and document-verification deadlines
  • Restrictions on representing multiple teams

False information, impersonation or the use of an ineligible player may lead to disqualification and disciplinary action.

03

Details to be confirmed

Teams and playing line-ups

Teams must submit their playing line-up within the period specified by match officials. Only eligible, officially listed players may participate.

  • Squad size and number of playing members
  • Deadline for submitting the final line-up
  • Permitted changes after submission
  • Injury substitutes and replacement players
04

Core match principle

The toss and the innings

A toss will be conducted before the match under the supervision of the appointed officials. The winning captain chooses whether to bat or field first.

Each team receives one innings of 50 legal deliveries. An innings ends when the allotted deliveries are completed, the permitted wickets are lost, the chasing team passes the target, or the innings ends under an applicable interruption or disciplinary provision.

05

Detailed conditions to be confirmed

Batting, dismissals and bowling

Runs may be scored through completed runs, boundaries and applicable extras. Recognised cricket dismissals may apply unless modified by the final SATAACK 50 regulations.

Bowling rules will be designed to encourage genuine team participation and prevent one bowler from dominating an innings.

  • Applicable modes of dismissal and retirement rules
  • Maximum deliveries permitted per bowler
  • Minimum number of bowlers required
  • Ball grouping or over structure
  • Incomplete spells and bowling changes
06

Detailed conditions to be confirmed

Extras, fielding and equipment

The final rulebook will define wides, no-balls, illegal deliveries, penalties and any additional-delivery or free-hit provisions. It will also establish any powerplay, fielding-circle or boundary-rider restrictions.

SATAACK 50 is played with a tennis ball. Approved ball specifications, bats, protective equipment, pitch dimensions, boundaries, stumps, clothing and venue-safety standards will be published before competition.

07

Confirmed result, procedures pending

Scoring, result and interruptions

Official scorers will record runs, wickets, legal deliveries, extras and individual performances. The team with the higher total wins, and the score approved by the match officials determines the result.

The Season 1 tie-break procedure, minimum requirements for a valid match, reduced-innings method, revised targets and abandonment conditions remain subject to final approval.

08

Competition standard

Player conduct and officials

Players and team personnel must compete hard, respect opponents and accept the authority of appointed officials. Abuse, intimidation, discrimination, deliberate time-wasting, result manipulation, betting-related misconduct and damage to property may result in penalties, suspension or disqualification.

Officials may inspect the playing area and equipment, judge deliveries and dismissals, manage interruptions, confirm the score and report misconduct. On-field decisions are final unless the published regulations expressly provide a review or correction process.

09

Administrative framework

Statistics, protests and amendments

Only performances from officially recognised matches count towards league records. The league may correct a record when supported by official scoring data, match footage or an officials’ report.

Administrative, eligibility and scoring protests must follow the published process and deadline. The league may amend its rules to protect safety, fairness or competition delivery, with material changes communicated through official channels.
